# Pitch-video narration draft · target 4–6 minutes (event maximum: 10 minutes)

KAPPA is an evidence-aware natural-product research workbench. Its purpose is not to declare a medicinal plant effective. It gives researchers a reproducible way to inspect distinct evidence records without blending botanical occurrence, traditional-use literature, biochemical assay measurements, model estimates and pharmacogenomic guidance into one claim.

First, what is not new. LOTUS, IMPPAT, NPASS, ChEMBL, Open Targets and NP-KG already do the heavy integration work, and a 2026 framework already links plant occurrence, scaffolds and ChEMBL evidence. We do not claim to be the first integrated natural-product platform. Our claim is narrower: an evidence contract, enforced in code, with an explicit refusal rule.

Our bounded pilot centers on the human PTGS2 biochemical IC50 endpoint. We retrieved 7,482 ChEMBL records and applied documented relation, unit, assay, species, quality-flag and structure filters. The resulting curated data contain 3,164 exact-structure representatives: 2,505 at or below one micromolar and 659 at or above ten micromolar. The intermediate band is omitted rather than called inactive.

We benchmark a class-weighted Random Forest and a logistic-regression baseline using a fixed scaffold-group holdout. The test set contains 702 structures and 82.6 percent are in the active band, which is also the approximate no-skill PR-AUC baseline. Random Forest ROC-AUC is 0.782 and PR-AUC is 0.940; balanced accuracy is 0.637. The apparent PR-AUC must be read against the high test prevalence. Probabilities are uncalibrated.

We also asked how much our duplicate-structure decision matters, because most activity models make that choice silently. Of 4,327 unique eligible structures, 371 carry more than one measurement and 32 carry both an active and an inactive one. Repeating the identical protocol with a median rule, and with conflicting structures excluded, gives held-out ROC-AUC of 0.774 and 0.825 against 0.782 for the primary rule, and balanced accuracy of 0.671 and 0.729 against 0.637. So ranking is moderately stable, while the threshold operating point is not. We keep the primary analysis and publish the sensitivity rather than picking the flattering number.

We tested more than one split. Training-only scaffold cross-validation and hyperparameter sensitivity are reported separately. A publication-document-held-out, scaffold-disjoint test gives ROC-AUC 0.724; a temporal, scaffold-disjoint internal test gives ROC-AUC 0.602. These are stress tests within the same ChEMBL target/source, not external validation. No independent assay, blinded study, wet-lab experiment or clinical validation has been performed.

The prediction interface shows a model-only activity-band estimate and a maximum Tanimoto similarity diagnostic. Similarity is not a reward and is not a calibrated confidence guarantee. The model-only triage value is withheld outside a training-derived reference range. We demonstrate that refusal: sucrose has a maximum training similarity of 0.193, below the 0.3236 reference, and the interface returns no triage value at all. Chemical identity and exact measured assay matches are presented separately. Curcumin is an exact training-structure match, so its output is retrospective, not prospective discovery.

The evidence explorer distinguishes the Wikidata statement that curcumin is found in *Curcuma longa* from a secondary ethnobotanical report. A 2012 review reports a cough-and-cold practice and cites a Malayalam-language book that we did not independently access. The entry lacks named community and precise locality context. It documents a literature report, not efficacy or safety, and is not mechanistically linked to curcumin or PTGS2. The record is also machine-validated: a fail-closed checker requires full attribution and limitations and rejects any claim that ties the plant practice to a compound, and the screen shows that coverage statement.

We also demonstrate a separate, verifiable pharmacogenomic evidence path. CPIC API records connect CYP2C19 with clopidogrel and guideline 100411. The CPIC pair is classified level A, and the guideline recommendations are displayed only for the cached intermediate- or poor-metabolizer records in the stated ACS/PCI context. ClinVar's c.681G>A, rs4244285 and *2 alias is used only for molecular identity; its cached germline label, likely benign for a not-specified trait, is a disease-association statement rather than a drug-metabolizer call, and we present it as such. A single *2 allele is not a complete diplotype or patient phenotype. This PGx graph component is deliberately disconnected from curcumin, plant use and PTGS2. No patient data or clinical recommendation is generated.

KAPPA is a research prototype. Data provenance, source terms, model validation, limitations and AI-assisted development disclosures are included in the project. The next scientific step is a genuinely independent, preferably blinded PTGS2 assay evaluation with protocol reconciliation, overlap checks, prevalence-aware PR metrics, calibration and domain-coverage reporting. Until that work is performed, the model output remains a bounded computational hypothesis—not a treatment claim.
