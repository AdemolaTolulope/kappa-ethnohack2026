# Limitations and non-use conditions

1. **Single target and endpoint.** The model classifies curated human PTGS2 biochemical IC50 bands. It is not a disease, selectivity, target-engagement, pharmacokinetic, toxicity, extract, or clinical-outcome model.
2. **Data source and assay shift.** ChEMBL data are literature-selected and assay conditions vary. Document-held-out and temporal/scaffold-disjoint tests show degraded performance; they are internal same-source stress tests, not external validation.
3. **Class imbalance and labels.** Overall positive prevalence is 79.17%, and test prevalence is 82.62%. PR-AUC must be read against the test prevalence baseline. The 1–10 µM grey band is omitted; binary labels are operational, not continuous potency.
4. **Calibration and applicability.** Probabilities are uncalibrated. The 0.3236 Tanimoto applicability threshold is a training-only relative similarity reference, not a validated confidence boundary. Exact training-structure predictions are retrospective.
5. **Chemical standardization.** RDKit canonicalization and exact-structure collapse do not imply comprehensive salt, tautomer, protonation, stereochemical or assay-condition harmonization. Duplicate collapse can affect publication-year fields.
6. **Ethnobotanical context.** One cough/cold practice is reported by a secondary paper that cites a Malayalam-language source not independently accessed. The entry lacks specific community/locality attribution. It documents a report, not efficacy or safety, and is not linked to curcumin or PTGS2. Further use requires source rights, community provenance, access and cultural review.
7. **Pharmacogenomics.** The CYP2C19–clopidogrel CPIC example is a separate published guideline demonstration. ClinVar is used only for variant/star-allele identity; CPIC recommendations are phenotype- and indication-conditioned. No patient genotype or curcumin/PTGS2 PGx association is represented.
8. **Importance and graph.** Permutation importance describes model reliance, not mechanism. Typed graph edges preserve evidence scope but do not establish causation. Similarity does not add positive evidence.
9. **No external or experimental validation.** No independent database/assay benchmark, prospective blinded evaluation, wet-lab experiment, clinical validation, or patient-level test has been performed.
10. **No clinical use.** Do not use KAPPA for diagnosis, treatment selection, clinical decision-making, patient response prediction, or safety decisions. A qualified researcher must inspect original source assays and independently validate hypotheses.
11. **Data and software rights.** Source data, software, publications and traditional knowledge each have their own terms. KAPPA's code licence does not relicense source data or grant cultural permission. Verify current terms before redistribution or deployment.
